Peak −60 mV · below threshold, no action potential

All-or-none

A neuron fires only when its membrane potential reaches threshold, around −55 mV. Below it, the change fades away. Above it, the response is always the same full spike, however strong the stimulus.

This is how nerves carry movement commands to muscles, and it is the principle behind electrical nerve stimulation such as TENS.

The gait cycle

One cycle runs from a heel strike to the next heel strike of the same foot. About 60% of it is stance, when the foot is on the ground, and about 40% is swing.

Gait training looks at how the hip, knee and ankle move through each phase, and where a patient's pattern differs from it.

Measuring hamstring length

The hamstrings run from the pelvis to just below the knee, so they cross both joints. With the hip held at 90°, straightening the knee stretches them.

How close the knee gets to straight is a common clinical measure of hamstring tightness, called the active knee extension test. Drag the foot or use the slider.
